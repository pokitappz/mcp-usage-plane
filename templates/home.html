The problem, measured

Counting HTTP requests overbills real MCP traffic by 2.98x.

MCP is not one request per billable unit. A multi round trip call is several requests and one result. Task polling is a dozen requests and one result. A failed call is a request and nothing delivered. Bill per request and the invoice is wrong in the customer's disfavour, which is the expensive direction to be wrong in.

That is a measurable claim, so it is measured. The comparison below runs against the strongest version of a request counter: one that reads Mcp-Name and applies your own price book per request, blind only to what came back.

What the free meter already does

The counting problem is solved, and it is not ours to sell back to you.

mcp-usage-kit records usage only after it observes a terminal result. Interim results bill nothing. Task creation and progress polls bill nothing. A completed task bills the originating tool once, keyed on its taskId. Errors, cancellations, discovery and subscription traffic are free. It is Apache-2.0, published on crates.io, and the conformance vectors are machine readable JSON that a gateway in any language can reuse.

If that is all you need, take it and go. It will not phone home, it has no licence key, and nothing here is gated behind an account.

What UsageKit Cloud is for

Correct counts are the easy half. The other half runs at 3am.

A meter produces numbers in a process. Turning those numbers into money that arrives, every month, without anyone watching, is a different system. This is that system, and it is what you would otherwise write.

A ledger that survives the process

Idempotent usage aggregates posted from any number of sidecars, deduplicated on their stable identifiers, in Postgres. Restart a sidecar mid batch and the counts do not move.

Exports that retry, and a queue when they cannot

A provider that is down does not lose a month of revenue. Permanent rejections land in a bounded dead letter queue that is visible in the dashboard, because silently dropped usage is silently dropped money.

Period close, not a per unit drip

Usage is charged once per closed period, in integer micros, with the floor and the included allowance applied in one place. A period cannot be charged twice however often the close runs.

Credentials sealed at rest

Your provider keys are sealed with ChaCha20-Poly1305 before they touch a row, and the export client refuses redirects so a credential cannot be carried to a host nobody reviewed.

Price books per customer

Tenants, their prices, their quotas and their spend caps, served to your sidecars as a snapshot they can cache. Your hot path keeps serving when this service does not.

A dashboard instead of a psql prompt

Usage by customer and meter, quota against limits, what each period was charged, and which exports failed. The numbers on the page are the numbers the API returns.

How it fits

Three steps, and your hot path does not depend on us.

  1. Meter where your server already is

    Add the Tower layer in a Rust MCP server, or run the mcp-usage-edge sidecar in front of a server written in anything else.

  2. Point it at the plane

    The sidecar pulls a snapshot of who may call and at what price, and posts idempotent aggregates back. Both directions are pull based and free of session state, so an outage here degrades to the last snapshot rather than to an error.

  3. Connect your provider

    The plane closes each period and submits the charge. You watch it in the dashboard rather than in a log.

Why this matters

Three ways billing goes wrong, and what each one costs.

MCP, the Model Context Protocol, is how AI assistants call your software. It does not map neatly onto requests, which is where the trouble starts.

A customer questions their bill

You cannot show them, line by line, what they were charged for. So you credit the invoice and hope it does not come up again. It usually does.

Somebody adds it up by hand

A query, a spreadsheet, a careful copy and paste into Stripe on the first of the month. It works right up until that person is on holiday or leaves.

Money quietly goes missing

Usage that fails to reach your payment provider does not look like an error. The work happened, the customer used it, and no invoice was ever raised for it.

The measurement

Charging per request overcharges by about three times.

One question from a customer is rarely one request. It can be a dozen: the assistant looks up what your tools do, asks a question, comes back for more detail, then waits while a long job finishes. A request that fails delivers nothing at all. Charge per request and you charge for every one of those.

What you get

Getting the number right is the easy part.

Counting correctly takes an afternoon. Making sure that count becomes money in your account, every month, without anyone remembering to check, is the part that takes a year and then wakes somebody up at 3am. That is what this is.

Every charge can be explained

Each billable event is stored with the customer it belongs to and when it happened. When someone questions an invoice you can answer from the record. Send the same usage twice, from any number of servers, and it still only counts once.

Failed charges do not vanish

If your payment provider is unreachable we keep trying. If it refuses something outright, that usage goes on a list you can actually see, because the alternative is revenue disappearing without anyone noticing.

One bill a month

Usage is charged once, after the month ends, rather than trickling out all month long. Running it twice cannot bill the same month twice, which matters the first time a deploy restarts halfway through.

Your payment keys stay secret

Your Stripe key is encrypted before it is written down and cannot be read back out, by you, by us, or by anyone holding a copy of the database. The security page names the algorithm.

Different prices for different customers

Set what each customer pays, how much they may use, and how much they may spend. Your own server keeps a cached copy of all of it, so if we have an outage your customers carry on being served.

You can see all of it

Usage by customer, who is near their limit, what each month was charged, and anything that failed to bill. The figures on screen are the same ones the API returns, because they come from the same query.

How it works

Three steps, and your customers never depend on us.

  1. Measure usage where your server already runs

    Add one line to a Rust server, or run a small proxy in front of a server written in anything else. For engineers: a Tower layer, or the mcp-usage-edge proxy.

  2. Point it at your control plane

    Your MCP server asks it who is allowed to call and what they pay, then reports back what was actually delivered. It keeps a cached copy of the answer, so a restart or a blip in the control plane leaves your customers being served rather than seeing an error.

  3. It turns that into invoices

    At the end of each month it works out what each customer owes and sends it to Stripe on your behalf. You watch that happen on a screen instead of reading a log file.

The honest part

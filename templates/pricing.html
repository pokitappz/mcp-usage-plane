The unit

A metered event is one terminal delivery, not one HTTP request.

This matters more than the number beside it, because it is the difference between a bill you can forecast and one that tracks your retry behaviour. The meter records an event only when it observes a result actually delivered.

What counts as a metered event

In the measured comparison on the home page, 45 HTTP requests produce 152 billable units and 0 metered events for everything that failed. Your event count is normally a fraction of your request count.

The fine print, in plain words

Is the meter itself free?
Yes, permanently. mcp-usage-kit and the mcp-usage-edge sidecar are Apache-2.0 on crates.io. They have no licence key, they do not report to us, and they work with no account. You are paying here for the hosted plane, and only if you want one.
What happens when I cross 50,000?
Events beyond the allowance are charged at the published rate. The allowance resets each month and applies to every account, so a month under 50,000 costs nothing even if last month did not.
How is it charged?
Once per closed month, not as a continuous drip. Each invoice records the terms it applied rather than pointing at your current terms, so a later price change never restates a bill you already received.
Do you meter my customers' data?
No. The ledger stores counts against opaque identifiers you choose. Tool names, prompt names and resource URIs never leave your server, by construction rather than by policy. See Security.
What if I want to leave?
Keep running the meter, which was never ours to take away, and point it somewhere else. The usage ledger is exportable and the integration contract is public, so nothing about this arrangement depends on it being hard to walk away from.

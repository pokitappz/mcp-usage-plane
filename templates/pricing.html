Why it is a flat number

Billing you per event would be a strange way to sell a meter.

Most billing infrastructure takes a slice of what you bill, which means your bill grows exactly when your margins are under most pressure, and it means the vendor has to see your numbers to charge you correctly.

We cannot see your numbers, because the software runs on your machines and reports nothing back. That rules out charging by volume, and honestly it is a better deal: one number you can put in a budget, which does not change when you have a good month.

There is no licence key, no check that phones home, and no audit clause. A deployment on your own servers cannot be policed, and pretending otherwise would only get in the way of the people who do pay.

The thing being counted

A billable event is one result your customer actually received.

You are not charged for these; this is what the software counts on your behalf, so that the invoices you send are right. It only counts an event once a result has genuinely been delivered.

Questions people ask

Do you host this?
Not at the moment. It is yours to run, which means no credential of yours reaches us, no data of yours sits on our machines, and you choose where it lives. If a hosted version is what you actually want, say so and it will count toward building one.
What counts as one deployment?
One production environment billing one set of customers. Development, staging and disaster-recovery standbys of that same environment are included. A second business line with its own customers is a second deployment.
Is the open source part really free?
Yes, permanently, and it is a different thing from this. The meter and the proxy that measure usage inside your own servers are Apache-2.0 with no licence key and no account. What costs $299 is the control plane around them: the record, the retries, the monthly run and the dashboard.
What happens if I stop paying?
Nothing switches off, because there is nothing in the software that could. You would be using it outside the licence, which is between you and your legal team rather than something we enforce technically. Whatever version you already hold still becomes Apache-2.0 four years after it shipped.
Can you see my customers' data?
No, and not as a promise: there is no path by which we could. The software talks to your database and your payment provider, and to nothing of ours. The security page shows exactly what it stores.
What if I want to leave?
Stop paying and stop using it, or keep the version you have until its Change Date and then carry on under Apache-2.0. Your data was never anywhere but your own database, so there is nothing to export and nobody to ask.

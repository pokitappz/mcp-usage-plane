To put that in proportion: if you charge your own customers two cents per call and you deliver 250,000 of them, you invoice $5,000 and this costs you $10. The whole point of the meter is that the $5,000 is the right number.

Above a million events a month the rate comes down. That one is worked out with you rather than published, because at that size the questions that matter are where your data lives and how long it is kept, not the unit price.

The thing being counted

A billable event is one result your customer actually received.

This matters more than the price next to it, because it is the difference between a bill you can predict and one that goes up when your network has a bad day. We only count an event once a result has genuinely been delivered.

In the measurement on the home page, 45 requests produce 152 billable events, and everything that failed produces none. Your event count is normally a fraction of your request count, which is why the price above is not the frightening number it looks like next to a request count.

Questions people ask

Is the open source part really free?
Yes, permanently. The meter and the proxy are Apache-2.0 and published for anyone to use. No licence key, nothing reported back to us, and no account needed. What you pay for here is the hosted service around it, and only if you want one.
What happens when I go over 50,000?
You are charged the published rate on everything above it. The free allowance resets every month, so a quiet month costs you nothing even if the one before it did not.
When am I charged?
Once, after the month ends. Each invoice records the prices that applied at the time rather than pointing at your current ones, so if we change our pricing later it never rewrites a bill you already received.
Can you see my customers' data?
No. We store counts against identifiers that you choose. The names of your tools, what was asked and what came back never leave your own servers, and there is nowhere in our database to put them. The security page shows exactly what is stored.
What if I want to leave?
Keep running the meter, which was never ours to take away, and point it somewhere else. Your usage history is exportable and the way the pieces talk to each other is published. Nothing about this arrangement relies on it being painful to walk away from.
